HOW FIGURE 4, LEGACY FIGURE 5, CURRENT FIGURE 5, AND FIGURE 6 WERE GENERATED


1. SHARED EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN AND DATA

All three figures use results from a full-factorial WaterSim5 experiment
containing 24,300 scenarios:

75 population-growth levels
 x 2 paired climate-related river-flow conditions
 x 2 environmental-flow levels
 x 3 municipal water-use-efficiency levels
 x 3 agricultural water-use-efficiency levels
 x 3 agricultural-to-urban water-transfer levels
 x 3 supply-augmentation levels
 = 24,300 scenarios.

The primary data file is data/merged_ob_data.csv.gz. For these analyses, the
scripts select the WaterSim output SINYRGWR in 2060. SINYRGWR is the
groundwater sustainability indicator (GSI), expressed as the number of years
groundwater pumping can be sustained. This produces one 2060 GSI value for each
of the 24,300 scenarios.

Scenario identifiers are decoded into the population-growth input (WEBPOPGR),
the paired Colorado River and Salt-Verde River flow adjustments, municipal
water-use efficiency, agricultural water-use efficiency,
agricultural-to-urban water transfer, supply augmentation, and
environmental-flow allocation.

WEBPOPGR is a population-growth scaling input rather than a population count.
The figures therefore use data/population_growth_data.csv.gz to translate each
WEBPOPGR level into regional population. The lookup selects provider-level
POPUSED in 2060 and sums it across the 33 modeled water providers. The main
experiment contains 75 WEBPOPGR levels from 2 through 150 in increments of two,
corresponding to approximately 4.1-9.2 million regional residents in 2060.

Excluding population, the factorial experiment contains 324 unique
climate-policy combinations. Each combination is represented at all 75 tested
population levels.


2. FIGURE 4: OVERALL POPULATION DISTRIBUTION AT EACH REALIZED GSI

Script: scripts/figure_4.R
Output: figures/figure_4.png

Figure 4 uses all 24,300 scenario-level GSI results in 2060 without fixing or
filtering any climate or policy factor. The code groups scenarios by their
exact realized GSI value. Thus, the group at GSI = 90 contains scenarios whose
2060 GSI equals exactly 90; it is not a minimum-threshold group and does not
include scenarios with GSI greater than 90.

Within each exact GSI group, the script summarizes WEBPOPGR using five
statistics:

- Minimum
- 25th percentile
- Median
- 75th percentile
- Maximum

The quantiles are R's standard type-7 sample quantiles. The number and
composition of scenarios can differ among GSI values because GSI is a modeled
outcome rather than an experimental input.

After calculating these statistics in WEBPOPGR units, the script converts each
summary value into 2060 regional population using the population lookup and
linear interpolation implemented by population_from_webpopgr(). It then orders
the summaries by realized GSI.

The figure plots:

- The x-axis as realized GSI in 2060, shown in years of groundwater supply.
- The y-axis as projected regional population in 2060, in millions.
- A light-blue ribbon from the minimum to maximum population at each GSI.
- A darker-blue ribbon from the 25th to 75th percentile.
- A red line showing the median population.

The plotted GSI range is approximately 45-100 years, with labeled ticks every
five years. The y-axis is displayed from 4 to 10 million people. Lines and
ribbons connect the summaries calculated for the observed GSI values; they are
not regression fits, smoothed response curves, confidence intervals, or
probability intervals.

Figure 4 asks:

    Across the complete factorial scenario ensemble, what population
    distribution is associated with each exact realized GSI value?

The full-range ribbon shows the lowest and highest tested populations found
among scenarios producing each GSI. The interquartile ribbon shows the middle
50% of their population distribution, and the red line shows the median. The
vertical width of a ribbon represents variation in population, not the number
of scenarios at that GSI. Because the figure conditions on the realized GSI
outcome, changes in its median and ribbon width can reflect changes in which
climate-policy combinations populate each GSI group.


3. LEGACY FIGURE 5: EXACT-GSI VIOLIN AND BOXPLOTS

Script: archive/legacy_figure_5/old_figure_5_violin.R
Output: archive/legacy_figure_5/old_figure_5_violin.png

The old Figure 5 contained four vertically arranged panels corresponding to
realized 2060 GSI values of exactly 70, 80, 90, and 100. For each panel, the
code retained scenarios using the literal filter:

    GSI value == panel value

There was no rounding, binning, tolerance, or greater-than-or-equal threshold.
For example, the GSI 90 panel contained only scenarios whose 2060 GSI was
exactly 90; it did not include scenarios with GSI greater than 90.

The y variable plotted by the code was WEBPOPGR, with axis labels translated
into the corresponding 2060 regional population. Each violin therefore showed
the distribution of population levels among scenarios that attained the exact
GSI value and satisfied the relevant policy filters.

Climate was shown using two adjacent colored violins at each policy level:

- Reference flows: Colorado River = 100% and Salt-Verde River = 105%.
- Reduced flows: Colorado River = 70% and Salt-Verde River = 85%.

The factor comparisons used progressively narrower background conditions:

- Environmental-flow allocation: compared 0 AF and approximately 80,000 AF;
  MWUE, AWUE, transfer, and augmentation varied.
- Municipal water-use efficiency: environmental flow was fixed at 0 AF;
  AWUE, transfer, and augmentation varied.
- Agricultural water-use efficiency: environmental flow was fixed at 0 AF
  and MWUE at the 45% reduction level; transfer and augmentation varied.
- Agricultural-to-urban water transfer: environmental flow was fixed at 0 AF,
  MWUE at the 45% reduction level, and AWUE at the current 2015 level;
  augmentation varied.
- Supply augmentation: environmental flow was fixed at 0 AF, MWUE at the 45%
  reduction level, AWUE at the current 2015 level, and transfer at 100%.

The violin width represented an estimated density of population values. The
violins used scale = "width," so their maximum widths were standardized and did
not represent the absolute number of qualifying scenarios. The embedded
boxplots showed the median and interquartile range, with standard boxplot
whiskers; outlier points were not drawn.

The old figure asked:

    Among scenarios that ended with a particular exact GSI value, what
    population levels occurred under selected policy and climate conditions?

It did not show the maximum population meeting a minimum GSI threshold or an
independent policy effect. Because the data were filtered on the realized GSI
outcome, factor levels could be represented by different numbers and
compositions of qualifying scenarios. The nested policy filters also meant
that the five factor comparisons did not share one common background design.
These features made comparisons difficult to interpret as direct effects.


4. CURRENT FIGURE 5: MEDIAN POPULATION FRONTIERS AND IQR RIBBONS

Script: scripts/figure_5.R
Output: figures/figure_5.png

The new Figure 5 organizes the 24,300 scenarios into 324 complete
climate-policy curves. Each curve contains the 75 tested population levels and
their corresponding 2060 GSI values. The code verifies that every one of the
324 combinations contains the complete ordered population experiment.

For each climate-policy combination and each integer GSI threshold from 79
through 98, the code identifies all tested populations satisfying:

    GSI >= threshold

The population frontier is the highest tested 2060 regional population among
those qualifying values. The code does not interpolate between population
levels or fit a response curve.

Each individual frontier is classified as:

- Observed within the tested range: at least one population meets the
  threshold, but the maximum tested population does not.
- Above the tested range: even the maximum tested population meets the
  threshold, so the true frontier exceeds the experiment's upper boundary.
- Below the tested range: even the minimum tested population fails the
  threshold, so the true frontier is below the experiment's lower boundary.

No censored frontier is replaced by the minimum or maximum tested population.
Instead, the code uses the known ordering of censored values to determine
whether the median, 25th percentile, and 75th percentile are exactly
identifiable. GSI 79-98 is the longest contiguous interval for which all three
quantiles are exactly identifiable for all 16 factor levels. At GSI 78, two
factor-level groups have a partly censored IQR; at GSI 99, two groups again
have a partly censored IQR; at GSI 100, the median itself is outside the tested
range for most groups.

The 324 frontier values at each threshold are then summarized separately for
the levels of six focal factors:

- Climate-related river-flow conditions
- Environmental-flow allocation
- Municipal water-use efficiency
- Agricultural water-use efficiency
- Agricultural-to-urban water transfer
- Supply augmentation

For climate and environmental flow, each factor level is summarized over 162
combinations of the other factors. For each three-level policy, each factor
level is summarized over 108 combinations of the other factors.

Within each panel:

- The x-axis is the minimum GSI threshold, 79-98 years.
- The y-axis is the maximum tested 2060 regional population meeting or
  exceeding that threshold, in millions.
- Each colored line is the median population frontier across the remaining
  climate-policy combinations.
- Each shaded ribbon is the 25th-75th percentile range of those frontiers.

The ribbons represent variation across factorial background combinations. They
are not confidence intervals, standard errors, or model-probability intervals.
The lines connect summaries calculated at the integer GSI thresholds and are
not fitted or smoothed statistical curves.

The new figure asks:

    Given a particular factor level, what is the typical maximum tested
    population that meets a minimum GSI requirement, and how much does that
    frontier vary across the other climate and policy combinations?

This corresponds more directly to the groundwater-constrained population
frontier concept than the exact-GSI conditioning used in the old Figure 5.


5. FIGURE 6: CONDITIONAL FEASIBILITY HEATMAPS

Current expanded script: scripts/figure_6.R
Current output: figures/figure_6.png

Figure 6 uses the same 24,300 scenario-level 2060 GSI results and the same
regional population lookup. Each panel fixes one level of one non-population
factor while allowing all other climate and policy factors to vary across
their full factorial combinations.

The figure contains panels for:

- Reference and reduced climate-related river-flow conditions
- 0 AF and approximately 80,000 AF environmental-flow allocation
- Three municipal water-use-efficiency levels
- Three agricultural water-use-efficiency levels
- Three agricultural-to-urban water-transfer levels
- Three supply-augmentation levels

At every one of the 75 population levels, a panel for a two-level factor
contains 162 combinations of the remaining factors. A panel for a three-level
factor contains 108 combinations of the remaining factors.

For every panel, every population level, and every integer GSI threshold from
60 through 100, the code calculates:

    100 x
    (number of remaining scenarios with 2060 GSI >= threshold)
    / (total remaining scenarios at that population and fixed factor level)

The denominator is always 162 in the climate and environmental-flow panels and
108 in the four three-level policy panels. The code verifies these denominators
before plotting.

Each panel contains 75 population rows x 41 GSI-threshold columns = 3,075
heatmap cells. Across the 16 factor-level panels, Figure 6 contains 49,200
cells. These are summaries of the same 24,300 model scenarios, not additional
model runs. A scenario can contribute to multiple thresholds: a scenario with
GSI = 92 counts as meeting thresholds 60 through 92 but not thresholds 93
through 100.

Within each heatmap:

- The x-axis is the minimum GSI threshold, 60-100 years.
- The y-axis is tested regional population in 2060, approximately 4.1-9.2
  million people.
- Cell color is the percentage of the remaining scenario combinations meeting
  or exceeding the GSI threshold.
- Lighter colors represent higher percentages; darker colors represent lower
  percentages.

For example, a cell in the AWT = 50% panel at a population of approximately
7 million and a GSI threshold of 90 reports the percentage of the 108
combinations of climate, environmental flow, MWUE, AWUE, and augmentation that
produced GSI >= 90 with AWT fixed at 50%.

Figure 6 asks:

    Given a specified level of one climate or policy factor, what percentage
    of the remaining factorial combinations supports a particular population
    while meeting a particular minimum GSI requirement?

The percentages describe conditional feasibility within the deliberately
constructed factorial scenario space. They are not probability estimates for
future outcomes. No GSI values are averaged: each scenario either meets or
fails the specified threshold.


6. RELATIONSHIP AMONG THE FOUR FIGURES

Figure 4 begins with an exact realized GSI and displays the overall population
distribution across the complete scenario ensemble at that GSI.

The old Figure 5 begins with an exact realized GSI and displays the population
distribution among scenarios that attained it.

The new Figure 5 begins with a minimum GSI requirement, calculates the maximum
qualifying population separately for every complete climate-policy
combination, and summarizes the distribution of those population frontiers.

Figure 6 begins with a population and minimum GSI requirement and reports the
percentage of the remaining climate-policy combinations that satisfies both.

In concise terms:

- Figure 4: overall population median, IQR, and full range conditional on exact
  realized GSI.
- Old Figure 5: population distributions conditional on exact realized GSI.
- New Figure 5: median and IQR of maximum qualifying population frontiers.
- Figure 6: conditional percentage of scenario combinations meeting each
  population-GSI requirement.
