MATCHED FRONTIER COMPARISON: SUMMARY, METHODS, AND FIGURE CAPTION


1. BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE ANALYSIS

This analysis measured how changing one climate or policy factor shifted the
groundwater-constrained population frontier while every other climate and
policy factor was held constant. The population frontier was defined, for a
given minimum groundwater sustainability indicator (GSI) threshold, as the
highest tested 2060 regional population whose modeled GSI met or exceeded that
threshold.

For each focal factor, scenarios were organized into matched backgrounds with
identical settings for all other non-population factors. Population frontiers
were then compared across the focal factor's levels within each matched
background. Pairwise differences were calculated as the frontier for the
second listed level minus the frontier for the first listed level. Positive
differences indicate expansion of the population frontier, negative differences
indicate contraction, and zero indicates that the factor-level change did not
move the frontier to a different tested population.

The analysis was restricted to GSI thresholds 83-93 because this was the
longest contiguous interval for which every frontier required for every matched
comparison was observed within the tested population range. The final figure
shows both the median signed frontier difference and how consistently the
matched backgrounds produced expansion, contraction, or no change.


2. METHODS

Matched comparisons of the groundwater-constrained population frontier

We conducted matched comparisons to quantify changes in the
groundwater-constrained population frontier associated with individual climate
and policy factors while holding all other non-population experimental factors
constant. The analysis used the 2060 groundwater sustainability indicator
(GSI) output from the complete 24,300-scenario factorial experiment and the
regional population associated with each of the 75 tested population-growth
levels. For each climate-policy combination and minimum GSI threshold, the
population frontier was defined as the highest tested 2060 regional population
whose GSI met or exceeded the threshold.

For each focal factor, a matched background contained identical levels of all
other non-population factors. For example, an agricultural-to-urban water
transfer comparison held climate-related river-flow conditions,
environmental-flow allocation, municipal water-use efficiency, agricultural
water-use efficiency, and supply augmentation constant while comparing the 0%,
50%, and 100% transfer levels. This procedure produced 162 matched backgrounds
per threshold for the two-level climate and environmental-flow factors and 108
matched backgrounds per threshold for each three-level policy factor.

A matched set was retained only when the population frontier was observed
within the tested population range for every level of the focal factor.
Frontiers below or above the tested population range were not assigned the
minimum or maximum population boundary. An identifiability analysis evaluated
all integer GSI thresholds from 45 through 100 and identified GSI 83-93 as the
longest contiguous interval with complete matched sets for all six factors.
Accordingly, the matched comparison was restricted to those thresholds.

Within each matched background, we calculated all pairwise factor-level
contrasts as the frontier for the second listed level minus the frontier for the
first listed level. For every contrast and GSI threshold, we summarized the
median frontier difference across matched backgrounds, expressed in millions
of people. We also classified each matched difference as an expansion
(positive), contraction (negative), or no change (zero). The most common of
these three responses and the percentage of matched backgrounds exhibiting it
were then calculated for each contrast and threshold. These percentages
describe consistency within the designed factorial scenario ensemble and are
not probabilities of future outcomes.
